Doing this on full-resolution raw pixels would melt even the largest supercomputers. Running thousands of neural passes over millions of RGB values is simply too heavy. In 2022, researchers discovered a brilliant shortcut: Latent Diffusion. Before adding any noise, a pre-trained encoder compresses the image down by a factor of eight, squeezing raw pixels into an abstract mathematical coordinate space called latent space. Inside this compact realm lives the U-Net. Shaped like a symmetrical hourglass, it crushes the noisy features down to understand the broad global composition, then expands back up, using skip connections to preserve razor-sharp microscopic details. The U-Net doesn't waste energy on imperceptible pixel noise. It sculpts meaning in the compressed language of pure concepts.
