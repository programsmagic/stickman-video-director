If you show a neural network pure static and demand, "Paint me an astronaut," it will fail every single time. The space of all possible images is astronomically vast, and guessing the right combination of millions of pixels is statistically impossible. But what if we don't ask it to paint the whole picture at once? What if we only ask it to undo the last half-second of noise? This is Langevin dynamics. At every step in the fog, the model acts like a mathematical compass. It calculates the Stein score—the vector pointing uphill toward higher probability density. Instead of guessing the final destination, the network only predicts the exact grain of noise that was added in the previous instant. Subtract that tiny spray of static, take one careful step down the gradient, and the fog begins to recede. Step by step, order crystallizes out of nothingness.
