To teach a machine how to create, we must first teach it how to destroy. Imagine our crisp image at timestep zero. We inject a microscopic pinch of random Gaussian noise—just a dusting of static. If we do this just once, the image is barely degraded. But repeat this gentle corruption one thousand times in a row, step by step, and something remarkable happens. The edges blur. The vibrant colors wash out into grey. By timestep one thousand, every single scrap of recognizable structure has vanished. The astronaut, the horse, the golden lighting—all dissolved into a sea of pure, mathematical white noise. In statistical physics, this is called a forward Markov chain. Destroying order is mathematically trivial. Any random particle collision can do it. The billion-dollar secret lies in what happens when you run the film in reverse.
