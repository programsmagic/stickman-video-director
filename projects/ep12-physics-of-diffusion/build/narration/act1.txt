Drop a single bead of midnight-blue ink into a beaker of clear water, and the laws of physics are ruthlessly clear. The ink molecules collide, scatter, and drift apart through Brownian motion. Within minutes, the structured droplet dissolves into a uniform, foggy haze. The Second Law of Thermodynamics dictates that entropy must always increase. In the real world, you cannot unspill coffee. You cannot unmix ink. Time only marches forward. But in 2015, a team of artificial intelligence researchers asked an audacious question: What if we could mathematically force the arrow of time to run backwards? What if, starting from nothing but pure thermodynamic chaos, a machine could unmix the noise and sculpt it back into art? Welcome to the physics of diffusion.
