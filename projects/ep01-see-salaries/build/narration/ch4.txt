Remember the number from the first morning? The one that was going to ruin your life? On day seven, you finally see it. It's above the quiet janitor in your building. The one who's worked there for thirty years. Nine point eight million. He bought the building's shares in the eighties. Saved every month. Never told anyone. Today, the dividends alone pay him more than everyone in the office combined. He waves at you. Same as always. At lunch, you sit next to him on the loading dock. You ask the only question that matters. Why are you still here? He shrugs. "I like the work. I like the people. And I never needed anyone to know my number." You ask him if he ever felt like giving up on saving. "Every month for thirty years, I put a little away. Most months, it felt like nothing. Then one day, I looked up, and it was everything." Then he gives you the only financial advice he's ever given anyone. "Spend less than you make. Own things that pay you. And stop looking at other people's numbers." It didn't ruin your life. It ruined the way you used to judge people.

At midnight, the numbers fade. The last one to go is your own. A week ago, that number felt like a grade. Like a score of how much you were worth. Now it just looks like what it is. A price someone agreed to pay for your time this year. It says nothing about your dad's cooking, your mum's notebook, or the janitor who waves every morning. Here's what seven days of seeing everyone's salary taught you. You can't read wealth from clothes. Not knowing what others earn can cost you real money, so ask. Comparing numbers can steal your happiness faster than any bill. And the number above someone's head is the least interesting thing about them.

Tomorrow, you wake up and see a different number. Not money. Karma.
