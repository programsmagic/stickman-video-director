For decades, mathematicians warned that deep neural networks shouldn't work. With billions of parameters, the loss landscape should be an impossible labyrinth of dead ends. Yet modern models find breathtakingly good solutions almost every single time. Why? Recent breakthroughs in high-dimensional topology revealed the secret: When you give an AI enough dimensions, the bad local minima don't just become rare—they vanish into connected global basins. The extra dimensions don't make the mountain harder to navigate. They carve escape tunnels through the fog. Gradient descent is not just an optimization algorithm. It is the universal cosmic chisel that carves raw chaos into structured understanding. Teaching sand to compute was the spark. Learning to navigate the geometry of loss... is how sand learned to think.
