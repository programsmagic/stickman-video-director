In machine learning, altitude equals mistakes. When a model is first born, its parameters are completely random. It sits at the highest, most chaotic peak of the mountain, making trillions of errors per second. The bottom of the valley is called the Global Minimum—the place where the AI's predictions match the real world. To get down, you might think you just follow the steepest slope downhill. But high-dimensional geometry plays cruel tricks on you. In three dimensions, you worry about getting trapped in a local pothole. But in ten thousand dimensions, potholes barely exist. Instead, you hit something far more sinister: Saddle Points. Just like a horse's saddle, the ground curves upwards in one direction, but downwards in another. If your algorithm hesitates, it stops moving completely. The gradient becomes zero. The machine falls asleep.
