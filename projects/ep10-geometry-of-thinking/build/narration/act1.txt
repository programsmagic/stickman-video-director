Imagine being dropped blindfolded onto the highest peak of Mount Everest in the middle of a pitch-black thunderstorm. Your goal is simple: Reach the bottom of the valley. You cannot see ten inches in front of your face. You have no map. And the mountain beneath your feet doesn't just have three dimensions—it has seven hundred billion dimensions. Every step you take could plunge you into a bottomless ravine or trap you on a dead-end plateau for eternity. This terrifying mountain is not science fiction. It is the exact mathematical reality that every artificial intelligence must navigate during training. Computer scientists call it the Loss Landscape. And how an AI descends this mountain is the single most important mathematical miracle of the modern world.
