For decades, humanity built systems of staggering intelligence, but we had to accept a terrifying Faustian bargain: we could use them, but we could not understand them.

A modern frontier language model contains hundreds of billions of numerical parameters. If you isolate a single neuron inside the network, you might hope it represents a clear, single concept—like a word, an object, or an emotion.

Instead, you encounter a baffling mystery known as polysemanticity.

A single neuron might fire enthusiastically when reading about the Golden Gate Bridge, academic footnotes, or lithium-ion batteries.

Why would a machine mix up such wildly different ideas?

The answer is superposition. Neural networks want to understand more concepts about reality than they have physical dimensions. To pack thousands of concepts into a smaller space, they store them in overlapping geometric angles—like multiple conversations whispered across the same crowded room.

To decode the mind of an artificial intelligence, we needed a prism capable of untangling the light.
