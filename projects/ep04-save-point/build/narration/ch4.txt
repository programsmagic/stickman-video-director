On day six, you decide not to touch the outside world. You stay inside. You spend the whole day with the people who matter most: your closest friend, your partner, your family. You have the most heartfelt, honest conversation of your life. Your friend cries. They tell you things they've never shared with anyone. You hug. It is the deepest human connection you've ever experienced.

Then, right before you go to bed, your friend accidentally knocks over your grandfather's vintage coffee mug. It shatters across the tiles. They look horrified, apologizing repeatedly. You look at the shards. You look at your wrists. It's so easy. Just two taps. The mug will be back on the shelf. You tap your wrists.

The mug is sitting safely on the counter. Two hours later, your friend comes over. You smile at them, feeling that warm, deep bond from yesterday. You bring up what they told you in tears. They look at you with total confusion. What are you talking about? I never said that.

A pit opens in your stomach. They didn't say that. Not in this reality. The breakthrough, the vulnerability, the tears, the sacred moment, it only happened in your private, erased memory. To them, it never existed. In erasing the broken mug, you erased the memory that bonded you together. You are now completely alone with memories no one else will ever share.
