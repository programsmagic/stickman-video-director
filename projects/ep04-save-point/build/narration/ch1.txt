You wake up at seven in the morning, and floating in front of your chest is a translucent menu. Save point created. At first, you think you're still dreaming. You swipe your hand through it, but your fingers buzz with a mild electric hum. Then small text appears beneath it: Tap your wrists together twice to reload. You look out the window. The streets are moving, buses are running, the world is completely normal. Except for you. For the next seven days, you have one active save point. No matter what happens, no matter what embarrassing mistake you make, what bad news you hear, or what disaster strikes, two taps of your wrists, and you're back in bed at seven o'clock this morning with full memory of everything that happened. At first, it feels like godhood. By day seven, it becomes the most terrifying prison ever created.

At first, it's just pure, unrestrained freedom. Because your whole life, fear has been your silent manager. Fear of looking stupid. Fear of rejection. Fear of losing money. At the coffee shop, the person you've had a crush on for six months is standing by the counter. Normally, your brain calculates forty-seven reasons to stay silent. Today? You walk right up, look them in the eye, and say the boldest, most direct compliment you've ever thought of. They blush. They smile. They hand you their number on a napkin. You walk outside, grinning from ear to ear.

Then you see your boss pulling into the office parking lot. You walk right up to him and tell him exactly what everyone in the department thinks of his Saturday emails. His face turns purple. He screams that you're fired. You smile. You tap your wrists together twice. Back in bed. Seven o'clock. The boss has no idea. The argument never happened. The world was reset, but the experience stayed in your head. Lesson one: living without fear feels like a superpower.
