## Script

### 1 · HOOK (0:00–0:35)
**[Midnight. A soft digital chime. Magi sits up in bed, rubbing his eyes. Floating in the air before him is a glowing prompt: "SAVE POINT CREATED: DAY 1 · 07:00 AM".]**
You wake up at seven in the morning, and floating in front of your chest is a translucent menu.
Save point created.
At first, you think you're still dreaming. You swipe your hand through it, but your fingers buzz with a mild electric hum.
Then small text appears beneath it:
"Tap your wrists together twice to reload."
You look out the window. The streets are moving, buses are running, the world is completely normal.
Except for you.
For the next seven days, you have one active save point.
No matter what happens—no matter what embarrassing mistake you make, what bad news you hear, or what disaster strikes—two taps of your wrists, and you're back in bed at seven o'clock this morning with full memory of everything that happened.
At first, it feels like godhood.
By day seven, it becomes the most terrifying prison ever created.
**[Scene text: "7 DAYS. ONE SAVE POINT. ZERO CONSEQUENCES."]**

### 2 · DAY 1 — THE CHEAT CODE
At first, it's just pure, unrestrained freedom.
Because your whole life, fear has been your silent manager. Fear of looking stupid. Fear of rejection. Fear of losing money.
**[Coffee shop. Barista is bustling. Magi steps up.]**
At the coffee shop, the person you've had a crush on for six months is standing by the counter.
Normally, your brain calculates forty-seven reasons to stay silent.
Today? You walk right up, look them in the eye, and say the boldest, most direct compliment you've ever thought of.
They blush. They smile. They hand you their number on a napkin.
You walk outside, grinning from ear to ear.
Then you see your boss pulling into the office parking lot.
**[Magi smirks, steps in front of the boss, and speaks his mind.]**
You walk right up to him and tell him exactly what everyone in the department thinks of his Saturday emails.
His face turns purple. He screams that you're fired.
You smile.
You tap your wrists together twice.
**[Screen flashes white. Snap sound. Magi sits up in bed at 7:00 AM.]**
Back in bed. 7:00 AM.
The boss has no idea. The argument never happened.
The world was reset, but the experience stayed in your head.
Lesson one: living without fear feels like a superpower.
**[Scene text: "CONSEQUENCES ARE JUST DATA."]**

### 3 · DAY 2 & 3 — THE CASINO AND THE PARALYSIS
By day two, the games get bigger.
**[Office computer screen: stock market graph jumping wildly.]**
You watch the opening bell. You note which stock spikes three hundred percent by noon.
Then you reload.
At 7:00 AM, you put your entire savings into that exact trade. By lunch, you're wealthy.
By day three, you've tried thirty-two different ways to handle your morning meeting until you deliver a presentation so flawless your manager gives you a standing ovation.
You feel invincible.
Until five o'clock on day three.
**[Magi stands alone in his living room, staring at his wrists. Clock ticking.]**
You're sitting on your couch, looking at your bank account, and something cold creeps into your stomach.
You want to order dinner, but suddenly, you hesitate.
"What if the pizza place is slow tonight? What if the sushi place is better?"
Normally, you'd just pick one, eat, and move on.
Now? You order pizza, eat two slices, tap your wrists, reload, and order sushi.
Then you reload again because you wonder if the Thai curry was the optimal choice.
Psychologists have a name for this: Maximization Paralysis.
When mistakes are impossible to undo, humans accept good-enough outcomes.
When every choice can theoretically be perfected, satisfaction drops to zero.
Because now, every minor imperfection is your fault for not resetting.
**[Scene text: "PERFECTION IS AN INFINITE LOOP."]**

### 4 · DAY 4 & 5 — THE BUTTERFLY AND THE STRANGER
On day four, reality stops being a game.
**[Street crossing. Rain pouring. Car skids on wet asphalt.]**
At 2:15 PM, you're walking across Fifth Street.
A delivery van runs a red light and clips a cyclist.
You scream, rush over, and see blood on the pavement.
Instinct kicks in. You tap your wrists.
**[White flash. 7:00 AM bed.]**
You wake up. You have seven hours to prevent it.
At 2:10 PM, you stand at that exact intersection. When the cyclist arrives, you step in front of him and ask for directions to delay him by thirty seconds.
He stops, points down the street, annoyed.
The delivery van speeds past through the intersection. No crash.
You breathe a sigh of relief. You saved him.
Until you turn the corner.
Because you delayed the cyclist, the delivery van entered the next intersection two seconds earlier than before.
Where it slammed directly into a crowded city bus.
Four people are injured.
**[Magi drops to his knees in shock.]**
You reload.
You wake up at 7:00 AM. Heart pounding. Sweating.
You try again. You pull the fire alarm in the coffee shop to delay the van driver.
That causes a traffic jam that blocks an ambulance three miles away.
No matter what you tweak, chaotic systems refuse to be tamed.
Edward Lorenz discovered this in weather models: in complex systems, eliminating one point of friction always displaces energy somewhere else.
By day five, you haven't slept. You've lived through Tuesday nineteen times.
You realize the horrible truth: you aren't playing god. You're just a frantic janitor trying to mop the ocean.
**[Scene text: "YOU CANNOT OUT-CALCULATE CHAOS."]**

### 5 · DAY 6 — THE GHOST IN THE ROOM
On day six, you decide not to touch the outside world.
You stay inside. You spend the whole day with the people who matter most: your closest friend, your partner, your family.
**[Warm kitchen. Magi sitting across from a friend sharing coffee.]**
You have the most heartfelt, honest conversation of your life.
Your friend cries. They tell you things they've never shared with anyone.
You hug. It is the deepest human connection you've ever experienced.
Then, right before you go to bed, your friend accidentally knocks over your grandfather's vintage coffee mug. It shatters across the tiles.
They look horrified, apologizing repeatedly.
You look at the shards. You look at your wrists.
It's so easy. Just two taps. The mug will be back on the shelf.
You tap your wrists.
**[White flash. 7:00 AM.]**
The mug is sitting safely on the counter.
Two hours later, your friend comes over.
You smile at them, feeling that warm, deep bond from yesterday.
You bring up what they told you in tears.
They look at you with total confusion.
"What are you talking about? I never said that."
A pit opens in your stomach.
They didn't say that. Not in this reality.
The breakthrough, the vulnerability, the tears, the sacred moment—it only happened in your private, erased memory.
To them, it never existed.
In erasing the broken mug, you erased the memory that bonded you together.
You are now completely alone with memories no one else will ever share.
**[Scene text: "SCARS ARE WHERE CONNECTION LIVES."]**

### 6 · DAY 7 — THE FINAL CHOICE (CLIMAX & RESOLUTION)
Day seven arrives.
**[Midnight approaches. The digital menu is flashing red: "SAVE POINT EXPIRES IN: 00:05:00".]**
The prompt is counting down.
You have five minutes to decide: do you reload one last time to your safety net, or do you let the clock strike midnight and surrender to the unknown?
You look at your hands.
For seven days, you tried to craft an existence without pain, without embarrassment, without heartbreak.
And what did you get?
Paralysis. Isolation. A life where nothing matters because nothing carries weight.
Psychologist Carl Jung wrote that to be alive is to bear the tension of opposites.
Courage only exists because danger is real.
Love only matters because loss is possible.
Joy only tastes sweet because bad days exist.
The menu flashes: "TEN SECONDS REMAINING. RELOAD?"
Magi puts his hands by his side.
He lets his wrists stay apart.
**[Clock strikes 12:00. The floating menu dissolves into golden dust.]**
The prompt vanishes.
Tomorrow will be messy. You might say the wrong thing. You might spill your coffee. You might get your heart broken.
And for the first time in seven days, you smile.
Because it's real.
**[Scene text: "PERFECTION IS DEAD. LIFE IS UNCERTAIN."]**
