The modern AI revolution did not start in a government supercomputer lab. It started on a gamer's graphics card.
In 2012, three researchers at the University of Toronto made a crazy bet.
Standard computer processors calculate one heavy equation at a time. But NVIDIA graphics cards are designed to calculate thousands of video game pixels at the exact same millisecond.
And rendering pixels turns out to be mathematically identical to multiplying matrices in neural networks.
When they ran AlexNet on two gaming GPUs, the error rate plunged from twenty-six percent to fifteen percent.
Deep learning had arrived.
