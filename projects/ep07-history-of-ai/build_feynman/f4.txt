Even with graphics cards, computers still read like anxious robots: word... by... word. By the time they reached the end of a long sentence, they forgot the beginning!
Then, in 2017, eight researchers at Google published a revolutionary paper: "Attention Is All You Need."
They invented the Transformer.
Instead of reading one word at a time, a transformer looks at every word in a sentence simultaneously.
Imagine the sentence: "The robot didn't cross the street because IT was too tired."
Who was tired? The street? Or the robot?
A human child instantly knows the robot was tired.
The transformer figures this out by shining invisible mathematical laser beams between all the words. The word "IT" shines its brightest laser beam directly at "ROBOT."
That laser connection is called an Attention Weight.
Scale that attention equation up with supercomputers, and you get modern language models like ChatGPT and Gemini that can write poems, explain homework, and understand code.
