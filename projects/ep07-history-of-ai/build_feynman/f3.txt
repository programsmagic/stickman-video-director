While the world gave up, a stubborn researcher named Geoffrey Hinton refused to quit.
Hinton believed neural networks weren't broken. They just needed two things: millions of real photos, and chips that could do thousands of math problems at the exact same second.
And then came an accidental discovery: video games!
To draw a 3D video game on your screen, a graphics card has to color two million pixels sixty times every second.
A normal computer processor is like a brilliant professor who solves one giant math problem at a time.
A gaming graphics card is like an army of thousands of painters who can all multiply colors at the exact same millisecond.
And coloring video game pixels turns out to be the exact same math as training artificial brain cells!
In October 2012, Hinton and his student Ilya Sutskever created an artificial brain called AlexNet, powered by two gaming graphics cards.
At a world contest identifying a million images, AlexNet destroyed every standard computer, dropping the error rate from twenty-six percent to fifteen percent.
The modern deep learning revolution was born.
