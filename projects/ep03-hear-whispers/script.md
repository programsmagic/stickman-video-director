## Script

### 1 · HOOK
**[Midnight. Clock ticks to 12:00. Soft echo. Magi wakes up, rubs eyes, hand to his ear.]**
You wake up, and the glowing numbers are gone.
Your head feels quiet. Normal, even.
Until you step out of your bedroom, close the front door, and take two steps down the hallway.
**[Soft whisper audio. Floating speech bubble: "Did he really wear that yellow scarf again?"]**
A voice. Faint, clear, and right in your ear.
Your roommate, talking inside the apartment you just left.
You freeze.
You didn't leave your phone on. You don't have an earpiece in.
For the next seven days, whenever you leave a room, you can hear every single sentence spoken about you.
Your friends. Your coworkers. Your family. The stranger you bumped into on the subway.
And by day seven, the quietest whisper in the city will change how you treat every person in your life.
**[Scene text: "7 DAYS. EVERY WHISPER."]**

### 2 · DAY 1 — THE HALLWAY
At first, your heart pounds through your ribcage.
Because your entire life, you've wondered: what do people actually say when the door closes?
**[Coffee shop. Magi grabs his latte, says thank you, and walks through the glass door.]**
You step outside the coffee shop. You listen.
**[Whisper: "That guy's wizard hat is kind of cool, honestly."]**
**[Barista: "He always orders the same thing, but he tips well."]**
Wait.
They're not mocking you.
In fact, after twenty seconds, they stop talking about you completely.
They're arguing about parking tickets.
Lesson one, before breakfast: people talk about you far less than you think.
Psychologists call this the spotlight effect. We live inside our own heads all day, so we naturally assume everyone else is thinking about us too.
In famous experiments at Cornell, researchers found people overestimate how much others notice them by more than double.
Most of the time, the moment you leave the room, you vanish from their minds.
And that's not insulting. It's a relief.
**[Scene text: "YOU'RE AN EXTRA IN EVERYONE ELSE'S MOVIE."]**

### 3 · DAY 2 — THE OFFICE
Then day two arrives, and you walk into work.
**[Office hallway. Desk with Kevin, boss's closed door.]**
At lunch, you stand up from the break room, excuse yourself to wash your hands, and linger outside the door.
**[Audio cut. Kevin's voice, muffled then clear: "Magi worked through the weekend on those charts. If he doesn't get credit, I'm bringing it up with HR."]**
Your jaw drops.
Kevin. The guy who barely says five words in morning meetings.
He isn't taking credit for your work. He's protecting you.
Researchers who study workplace behaviour found something fascinating:
We assume workplace gossip is always malicious — people tearing each other down behind closed doors.
But studies by organizational sociologists found that over seventy percent of everyday office chatter is neutral, and a huge chunk is what scientists call "prosocial gossip."
People sharing information to protect coworkers and enforce fairness.
Kevin wasn't gossiping to hurt anyone. He was watching your back.
**[Magi outside the door, smiling softly. Scene text: "THE PEOPLE WHO DEFEND YOU IN ROOMS YOU'RE NOT IN."]**

### 4 · DAY 3 — THE RUMOR MACHINE
On day three, you hear the dark side.
**[Water cooler. Two stick figures whispering: whisper-a and whisper-b.]**
You drop your pen in the corridor, step around the corner, and listen.
One coworker tells another: "I heard Magi was ten minutes late yesterday because he got stopped by security."
Ten minutes later, you hear that same coworker talking to someone on the fourth floor:
"Magi had an incident with building security yesterday. Something about his pass being revoked."
By 3:00 p.m., the whisper reaching your ear is completely unhinged:
"Did you hear Magi got escorted out by guards?"
You stand by the elevators, stunned. You weren't late. You stopped to tie your shoelace.
How did tying a shoelace turn into armed guards?
In 1932, psychologist Frederic Bartlett ran experiments on how stories travel from person to person.
He found that rumors mutate through three predictable stages:
Leveling — people drop the boring details.
Sharpening — people exaggerate the dramatic parts.
And assimilation — people twist the story to fit what they already believe.
Every time a sentence travels behind your back, it loses truth and gains drama.
The whisper wasn't about who you are. It was about human entertainment.
**[Doodle: a tiny shoelace evolving into a giant dragon. Scene text: "RUMORS DON'T CARRY TRUTH. THEY CARRY DRAMA."]**

### 5 · DAY 4 — THE STAB THAT HURTS
On day four, you hear the sentence you were dreading.
**[Restaurant table. Magi sitting across from a close friend. They laugh. Friend hugs him goodbye.]**
You walk out to the car park.
Your ears buzz. The whisper clicks on.
**[Audio: friend's voice on speakerphone to someone else: "I love Magi, but sometimes he's just exhausting to be around. He overthinks everything."]**
It hits like ice water down your neck.
Your hands grip the steering wheel. Your stomach knots.
Exhausting? Overthinks everything?
You've known this person for seven years. You helped them move apartments. You listened to their breakups.
For three hours, you sit in your car, replaying those eight words on repeat.
Psychologists call this the negativity bias.
Our ancestors survived by paying ten times more attention to danger than to safety. A predator in the bushes matters more than a ripe berry on a branch.
So your brain treats one piece of negative gossip like a life-or-death wound, while completely erasing a hundred kind things.
Then you realize something painful.
Are you exhausting sometimes? Do you overthink?
Yes. You do.
A cruel sentence spoken behind your back hurts. But sometimes, buried underneath the sting, it's the only mirror honest enough to tell you what no one will say to your face.
**[Magi in the car, looking in the rear-view mirror. Scene text: "CRITICISM CAN STING AND STILL BE TRUE."]**

### 6 · DAY 5 — WHY WE WHISPER
By day five, the whispers are driving you crazy.
**[Montage: crowded train, gym, street corner. Floating whisper clouds.]**
Every time you leave a room, someone says something.
A jogger commenting on your pace. A cashier remarking on your change.
Why do humans do this? Why can't anyone just keep their thoughts to themselves?
It turns out, evolutionary anthropologists studied this.
Robin Dunbar, the famous Oxford anthropologist, discovered that roughly sixty-five percent of all human conversation across cultures is gossip — talking about who did what, who is trustworthy, and who broke a rule.
In primates, bonding is done by physical grooming — picking bugs out of fur for hours.
Humans formed tribes too large for grooming. So we evolved language, and language became social grooming.
Gossip isn't a modern flaw of bored people. It was the original social glue that kept ancient tribes alive.
Without it, no one would know who to trust.
The problem isn't that people talk behind your back.
The problem is that you were never biologically equipped to hear it.
**[Doodle: two monkeys grooming → two stick figures talking over a fence.]**

### 7 · DAY 6 — THE DEFENDER
On day six, you hear the whisper that changes everything.
**[Office break room. A difficult client and a manager talking.]**
You step into the hallway to make a phone call.
A sharp voice cuts through your ears.
**[Client voice: "Honestly, that Magi guy seems in over his head. We should ask for someone else on this account."]**
Your heart sinks. Your chest tightens.
You reach for the door handle, ready to walk in and apologize or defend yourself.
And then another voice speaks.
**[Calm, steady voice: "That's not fair. Magi spent three weeks fixing the data errors your team missed. If there's an issue with this account, it's on my desk, not his."]**
You stop.
It's your boss. The one who never gives compliments. The one who sends one-word emails and looks bored in presentations.
He didn't know you could hear him. There was no audience. No cameras. No karma score floating above his head.
He defended your name when you weren't in the room to defend yourself.
There is no praise on earth sweeter than the praise you were never supposed to hear.
And you realize something profound:
You've spent your entire life worrying about the people who talk about you when you leave.
When the only people who actually matter are the ones who don't let anyone tear you down while you're gone.
**[Scene text: "CHARACTER IS WHAT YOU SAY ABOUT SOMEONE WHEN THEY CAN'T HEAR YOU."]**

### 8 · DAY 7 — THE SILENCE
On day seven, you decide to test something.
**[Street, coffee shop, apartment hallway.]**
All day, you deliberately don't listen.
When you step out of a room, you don't wait for the click. You don't strain your ears.
You walk forward.
You focus on what you're doing, who you're helping, and the person standing right in front of you.
And a strange peace settles in.
Because you finally understand the secret.
What people say about you behind your back is almost never about you.
It's about their own insecurity. Their own boredom. Their need to bond with whoever is standing next to them.
When someone speaks ill of you, they aren't describing your character. They're revealing their own.
And when someone speaks well of you in an empty room, that's not reputation. That's love.
**[Magi walking down a quiet street at sunset, scarf blowing gently.]**

### 9 · MIDNIGHT
At 11:59 p.m., the clock approaches midnight.
**[Bedroom. Mirror. Clock on the bedside table.]**
The whispers grow soft. Then faint. Then completely silent.
The superpower is gone.
You stand in the quiet room, looking at your reflection.
No numbers above your head. No voices in your ears.
Just you.
Here is what seven days of hearing every whisper taught you:
Most people aren't talking about you at all.
Rumors are just stories told by bored minds.
Criticism can hurt and still have a seed of truth.
And the truest measure of friendship isn't who smiles in your face — it's who defends your name when the door closes.
So tomorrow morning, when you walk into a room and someone leaves to grab coffee…
Remember what it felt like to be on the other side of the door.
**[Scene text: "BE THE PERSON WHO DEFENDS SOMEONE IN AN EMPTY ROOM."]**

### 10 · END CARD
Tomorrow, the silence continues.
Until you look at someone's face, and you see a tiny digital clock ticking down on their forehead.
Years, days, hours, seconds.
**[Street scene: stick figures with countdown timers above their eyebrows. Magi looks startled.]**
What If You Could See Everyone's Lifespan?
If you want to know what happens when the clock hits zero, subscribe. Because tomorrow, the countdown begins.
**[Scene text: "NEXT: WHAT IF YOU COULD SEE EVERYONE'S LIFESPAN?"]**
