By day five, the whispers are driving you crazy. Every time you leave a room, someone says something. A jogger commenting on your pace. A cashier remarking on your change. Why do humans do this? Why can't anyone just keep their thoughts to themselves? It turns out, evolutionary anthropologists studied this. Robin Dunbar, the famous Oxford anthropologist, discovered that roughly sixty-five percent of all human conversation across cultures is gossip: talking about who did what, who is trustworthy, and who broke a rule. In primates, bonding is done by physical grooming, picking bugs out of fur for hours. Humans formed tribes too large for grooming. So we evolved language, and language became social grooming. Gossip isn't a modern flaw of bored people. It was the original social glue that kept ancient tribes alive. Without it, no one would know who to trust. The problem isn't that people talk behind your back. The problem is that you were never biologically equipped to hear it.

On day six, you hear the whisper that changes everything. You step into the hallway to make a phone call. A sharp voice cuts through your ears. Honestly, that Magi guy seems in over his head. We should ask for someone else on this account. Your heart sinks. Your chest tightens. You reach for the door handle, ready to walk in and apologize or defend yourself. And then another voice speaks. That's not fair. Magi spent three weeks fixing the data errors your team missed. If there's an issue with this account, it's on my desk, not his. You stop. It's your boss. The one who never gives compliments. The one who sends one-word emails and looks bored in presentations. He didn't know you could hear him. There was no audience. No cameras. No karma score floating above his head. He defended your name when you weren't in the room to defend yourself. There is no praise on earth sweeter than the praise you were never supposed to hear. And you realize something profound: You've spent your entire life worrying about the people who talk about you when you leave. When the only people who actually matter are the ones who don't let anyone tear you down while you're gone.
