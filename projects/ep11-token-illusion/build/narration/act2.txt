Ask an early state-of-the-art language model: "How many R's are in strawberry?" And with supreme confidence, it will look you in the eye and say: "Two." Why does a trillion-parameter mind that can pass the bar exam fail a first-grade spelling test? Because the model never saw the letters in strawberry. Its tokenizer chopped the word into two solid blocks: "straw" and "berry". Inside the model's vector space, "berry" is an atomic concept associated with fruit, sweetness, and jam. It doesn't have an internal spelling ledger. To count the R's, the model has to mentally guess what characters might make up that token—a blind deduction, like asking a human to describe the atomic weight of a brick without weighing it.
