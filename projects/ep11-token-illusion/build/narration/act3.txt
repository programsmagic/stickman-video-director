Why not just feed raw letters into the AI? Because human language is wildly inefficient. If an AI read one letter at a time, a single paragraph would require thousands of attention calculations, causing processing costs to explode quadratically. In 2016, researchers adapted a nineteenth-century data compression algorithm called Byte-Pair Encoding, or BPE. The tokenizer scans billions of web pages and counts which character pairs appear most frequently. First, "e" and "r" merge into "er". Then "t" and "h" merge into "th". After millions of iterations, common words like "the", "programming", and "extraordinary" earn their own dedicated locker in a vocabulary of one hundred thousand tokens. Rare words get sliced into syllables; common words travel as a single bullet.
