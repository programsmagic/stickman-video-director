When you type a sentence into ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude, you assume the machine reads the letters just like you do. You imagine a digital eye scanning the characters: S, T, R, A, W, B, E, R, R, Y. In reality, modern artificial intelligence is completely, utterly blind to letters. Before a neural network ever sees your prompt, your text is fed into a high-speed linguistic guillotine called a Tokenizer. This machine slices your words into chunky, irregular fragments called tokens. To the AI, words don't exist. Letters don't exist. There are only numbers stamped onto jigsaw blocks. And this hidden layer of illusion is the reason why the smartest supercomputers in history stumble on the simplest children's riddles.
