Before 2017, AI had a terrible memory problem. Models read sentences one word at a time, like a tired reader looking through a drinking straw. By the time they reached the end of a long paragraph, they completely forgot what happened at the beginning! Then, eight researchers at Google published a paper with an audacious title: Attention Is All You Need. Instead of reading sequentially, what if the model could look at every single word in an entire document simultaneously, in parallel? This was the invention of the Transformer, and it changed everything.

How does a Transformer know which words belong together? Through an elegant mathematical dance called Query, Key, and Value. Imagine every word in a sentence is at a crowded cocktail party. The word it holds up a megaphone called a Query: Who does the word it refer to? Every other word wears a name badge called a Key. The word socket says: I am a wall outlet. The word robot says: I am a mechanical machine that was tired. The math multiplies the Query by every Key simultaneously. Socket gets a twelve percent relevance score. But robot scores an eighty-eight percent match! Because of that high score, robot opens its briefcase of information — the Value — and hands it straight to it. Now, the model knows exactly what it means!

Modern models don't just use one spotlight. GPT-4 runs ninety-six attention heads simultaneously! One flashlight tracks grammar, another tracks emotional tone, another connects pronouns, and another tracks technical facts. Once all ninety-six flashlights finish analyzing every word in parallel, the Transformer does something deceptively simple: it calculates a probability for what single word should come next. Eighty-nine percent chance it's recharged, seven percent exploded, zero-point-zero-zero-one percent danced. It picks the best option and repeats the loop. This is Species One: the fast, conversational chatbot.
