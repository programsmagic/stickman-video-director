Computers cannot read letters. A computer chip is literally just billions of microscopic switches that are either ON or OFF — ones and zeros. It has no idea what the letter A or the word strawberry means. In fact, this explains the most famous AI bug in history! When you ask an older chatbot how many r's are in strawberry, it proudly says Two! Why? Because the AI never saw the individual letters! A mechanical tool called a Tokenizer chopped the word into puzzle blocks using a process called Byte-Pair Encoding. The word strawberry becomes three separate tokens: S-T-R-A-W, then A-W, then B-E-R-R-Y. The model knows berry has two r's, but straw was just an opaque number! If it can't see the letters, how does it understand what words mean?

Enter the miracle of Embeddings! Imagine a colossal supermarket with thousands of aisles. The store manager doesn't shelve items alphabetically. Bananas don't sit next to Band-Aids just because they both start with the letter B! Bananas sit next to apples, oranges, and mangoes in the fruit aisle. And dog food sits next to leashes in the pet aisle. In machine learning, this is called an Embedding Space. In models like GPT-4, every single token is given a GPS coordinate made of over twelve thousand numbers! Each number represents a different dimension of meaning: sweetness, furriness, historical era, or emotional warmth.

Because words are now geometric coordinates, you can literally do algebra with human thoughts! If you take the GPS coordinates of the word KING, subtract the vector for MAN, and add the vector for WOMAN... the coordinates glide straight across the supermarket space and land directly on the shelf for QUEEN! The computer doesn't know what it feels like to wear a crown. It just knows multidimensional geometry! And in modern Transformers, these coordinates are alive: if the word bank sits next to river, its vector steers toward water; if it sits next to robbery, it steers toward cash.
