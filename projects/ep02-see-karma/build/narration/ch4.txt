Remember the lowest number in the city? The one you were never supposed to expect? On day seven, you finally find it. It's above a woman in a grey uniform, standing next to a parking meter. Minus four thousand, one hundred and twenty. She's a parking officer. Every day, people curse at her, slam their car doors, and wish her the worst. But here's the thing. Her number isn't low because of what she's done. It's low because of what everyone else has said to her. And when you look closer, the number flickers. Underneath, faint and small, there's a second one. Plus three thousand, six hundred. Her real karma. The big red number was never hers. It was ours. You walk over, and you tell her she's doing a hard job well. She laughs. She says nobody has said that to her in eleven years. Then she tells you about a man who once thanked her for a ticket, because it reminded him to fix his brakes before a long drive with his kids. She still remembers his face.
