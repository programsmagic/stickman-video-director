You wake up, look in the mirror, and there's a number above your head again. But this time, it isn't money. Plus seventy-three. It's your karma. Every kind thing you've ever done, minus every cruel one, added into a single score. Everyone has one. For the next seven days, you can see exactly how good, or how bad, everyone really is. And by day seven, the lowest number in the city will belong to someone you'd never expect.

At first, it's just fascinating. The barista who remembers everyone's name is at plus four hundred and ten. The man yelling at his phone is at minus thirty-eight. Fair enough. But then you see the man in the expensive suit. The one who looks like he'd step over you to catch a taxi. Plus two thousand, three hundred. You don't know what he did. You'll never know. But he did a lot of it. And the little girl holding her mum's hand? Plus ninety. Already beating you. Lesson one, before breakfast: just like money, you can't see goodness from the outside.

Then you walk into work. And there's Kevin. Kevin, who forwards your emails and calls it teamwork. Kevin is at plus eight hundred and eighty. That's more than ten times your number. You can't stop staring, so at lunch, you ask around. Kevin covers night shifts for a coworker whose kid is sick. Every week. He never mentioned it. You ask him why he never told anyone. He shrugs. "Didn't seem like the kind of thing you announce." You've spent years deciding who Kevin was from the tiny part of him you could see. You were wrong about his salary last week. You were wrong about this, too.
