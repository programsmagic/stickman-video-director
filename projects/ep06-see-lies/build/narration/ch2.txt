By noon on day one, you are sitting in a team meeting at work.
Your manager clicks to the next slide and says, I really appreciate everyone's extra hours on this launch.
Crimson tag: Lie. He only cares about his quarterly bonus.
A colleague leans over and whispers, don't worry, your presentation was fantastic.
Crimson tag: Lie. She did not listen to a single word.
Psychologist Robert Feldman discovered that in an ordinary ten-minute conversation, the average human tells two to three lies.
We do not do it because we are monsters.
We do it because radical honesty is psychological warfare.
Imagine if everyone said, I am bored of talking to you.
Your new haircut looks terrible.
I only invited you because I felt obligated.
Civilization survives on tiny white lies like an engine survives on motor oil.
Without that oil, human conversation grinds together until the gears shatter.
By the end of your second day, you are terrified to speak.
Because every time someone is nice to you, the glowing red badge tells you:
They are only trying to survive the interaction.
