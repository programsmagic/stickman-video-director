By day four, the small white lies stop bothering you.
It is the deeper lies that begin to tear you apart.
You meet your closest friend for coffee.
You ask him how he has been doing since his breakup.
He smiles and says, honestly, I am completely over it. I feel freer than ever.
Above his chest, a soft red glow pulses: Lie. He cries himself to sleep every night.
Here, the crimson tag is not malice. It is shame.
Humans lie to hide their bleeding wounds.
We lie so our friends will not see how fragile we really are.
Then comes the real shock.
You ask him, are we good? You felt distant lately.
He pauses, looks at the table, and says:
I was envious of your promotion. And I hated myself for feeling that way.
Above his words, there is no red tag.
A soft emerald light illuminates the air: Truth. One hundred percent.
It hits you like a physical shock.
Hearing an ugly truth feels ten times more loving than hearing a beautiful lie.
Because for the first time in your life, you are meeting a human being without armor.
