On the evening of day six, you walk back to your apartment in complete silence.
You realize that you have spent five days judging the world.
You analyzed your boss. You tested your friends. You graded every stranger on the subway.
So you walk into your bathroom.
You lock the door.
And you look straight into your own reflection.
I am happy with where I am in life, you whisper.
Crimson flash: Lie.
I do not care what people think of me.
Crimson flash: Lie.
I have forgiven everyone who hurt me.
A violent red pulse: Lie.
The tears start running down your cheeks.
The most dangerous deceiver in your life was never your coworker or your partner.
It was you.
We tell ourselves comfortable fairy tales so we do not have to face our fear of failure, our grief, and our loneliness.
You stare into the mirror and finally say the one thing you have avoided for ten years:
I am terrified of being alone. And I am trying my best.
For the first time all week, the crimson vanishes.
A deep, tranquil green envelops the glass: Truth. Absolute.
