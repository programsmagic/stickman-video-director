# EP01 — What If You Could Hear Everyone's Thoughts for One Day?

**Series:** "One Day With…" — each episode, the same stickman (you) gets one strange power from midnight to midnight. One power, one day, one lesson grounded in real science.

## Script

### 1 · HOOK (0:00–0:30)
**[Black ink line draws a clock hitting 12:00. A soft "click".]**
At midnight, the world gets loud.
**[Stickman in bed, eyes snap open. Hundreds of tiny thought bubbles bloom outside the window.]**
Not your phone. Not the street.
Everyone's thoughts.
**[Bubbles fill the screen: "did I lock the car?" "pizza." "why did I say that in 2014"]**
For the next twenty-four hours, you can hear what every person near you is thinking.
Your boss. Your crush. The stranger on the train.
And by the end of the day, one thought will change how you see everyone.
**[Freeze on one bubble, face-down, unreadable. Scene text: "ONE DAY. EVERY THOUGHT."]**

### 2 · 7:00 AM — THE FLOOD
You wake up, and your neighbour is already thinking about toast.
**[Wall cutaway: neighbour stickman with a toaster, bubble: "toast… toast… toast."]**
Then the jogger outside. Then the bus driver. Then forty people at the bus stop, all at once.
Scientists at Queen's University tried to count how often a new thought starts. Their estimate: around six thousand times a day, for each person.
**[Counter ticks up: 6,000+ / day. Then ×40 people. The stickman's scarf flaps like he's in a storm.]**
So a crowded bus isn't a conversation. It's a stadium.
**[Stickman squeezed into a bus, bubbles overlapping into a black scribble.]**

### 3 · 8:30 AM — THE SILENT ONES
But here's the strange part. Not everyone is loud.
**[Bus pans past passengers; one woman's head is calm, no bubble — just a tiny drawn image of a cat.]**
Some people barely hear words in their heads at all. Researchers recently gave this a name: anendophasia — little or no inner voice.
They still think. Just in pictures, feelings, and ideas instead of sentences.
**[Her bubble fills with sketches: a cat, a key, a sun.]**
And even talkative minds aren't talking all the time. In studies where people were beeped at random moments, inner speech showed up far less than most of us assume.
So on day one of mind-reading, you learn something no one expects: most heads are quieter than yours.
**[Stickman, surprised, looks around. Scene text: "QUIETER THAN YOU THINK."]**

### 4 · 9:15 AM — NOBODY IS LOOKING AT YOU
You walk into work with a coffee stain on your shirt.
**[Close-up: brown splash on the stickman's chest. He covers it with one hand, sweating.]**
Your heart is pounding. Everyone must see it.
You listen.
**[Coworkers' bubbles: "is it Friday?" "my back hurts" "did I reply to that email?"]**
Nobody noticed. Not one person.
Psychologists call this the spotlight effect. In a famous experiment, students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room. They guessed about half the people noticed. In reality, it was closer to a quarter.
**[Two bars: "you think 50%" vs "actually ~25%". The coffee stain shrinks.]**
Everyone is the main character of their own head. You're an extra.
And honestly? That's freedom.
**[Stickman lowers his hand, shoulders relax.]**

### 5 · 12:30 PM — THE THOUGHT THAT HURTS
Lunch with your best friend.
They're smiling. They're nodding.
And then you hear it.
**[AUDIO CUT: background stops. One bubble, red marker: "ugh, he's talking about this again."]**
It lands like a punch.
**[Stickman's sandwich drops. Mouth falls open.]**
But wait. One second later:
**[Friend's next bubble: "…I'm so tired. I hope he's okay though."]**
Here's what nobody tells you about minds: they say things we don't mean.
In a study across thirteen countries, almost everyone reported having unwanted, intrusive thoughts — random, rude, even disturbing ones they'd never act on.
**[Map with small dots across continents. Scene text: "~94% OF PEOPLE."]**
A thought isn't a decision. It's weather.
You'd never want someone to judge you by your worst passing thought. So maybe don't judge them by theirs.
**[Stickman picks up the sandwich, offers half to the friend.]**

### 6 · 3:00 PM — THE TEMPTATION
By the afternoon, you realize what you could do with this.
**[Montage: poker table, job interview, an exam.]**
Win every card game. Ace every interview. Know exactly what to say to anyone.
**[Stickman grinning, leaning over a poker table, cards fanned.]**
But every shortcut has a cost.
You stop listening to what people say — only to what they think.
And conversations start to feel like cheating.
**[The friend from lunch talks; stickman stares at the bubble instead of their face. Scene text: "LISTENING ≠ HEARING."]**

### 7 · 6:00 PM — THE OVERLOAD
Rush hour.
**[Train station. Bubbles everywhere, overlapping, scribbling into black.]**
Thousands of minds. Worries, songs stuck on repeat, grocery lists, arguments with people who aren't even there.
Your brain was never built to carry everyone's inner world at once.
**[Stickman sits on a bench, hands on ears, scarf drooping.]**
For the first time, you wish you could hear… nothing.

### 8 · 8:00 PM — THE ONE THOUGHT
You go home. Dinner with your mum.
**[Kitchen. Quiet. Just two stick figures at a small table.]**
She asks how your day was.
You say "fine."
And then you hear her thought.
**[One soft bubble, yellow marker: "He looks tired. I wish he'd tell me what's wrong."]**
She doesn't say it. She just passes you the salt.
**[Close-up: her hand sliding the salt across. A small "tap".]**
Of all the thoughts you heard today, this is the one that stays.
Because it means the people around you are often thinking more kindly than they ever say out loud.
**[Stickman looks up. Puts his fork down.]**
So you tell her what's wrong.

### 9 · 11:59 PM — SILENCE
The clock ticks toward midnight.
**[Same clock as the opening. Bubbles outside the window fading one by one.]**
The noise fades.
You're back to hearing only one mind. Yours.
Here's what one day of mind-reading taught you:
Most people aren't watching you. Most cruel thoughts are just noise. And the kind ones are usually there — they just never get said.
So maybe the real superpower isn't hearing thoughts.
It's saying the good ones out loud.
**[Stickman in bed, smiling. Scene text: "SAY THE GOOD ONES."]**

### 10 · END CARD (≈10 s)
Tomorrow at midnight, you get a new power.
You can see ten seconds into the future.
**[Clock strikes 12. Stickman's eyes glow with a tiny forward arrow.]**
What could possibly go wrong?
**[Scene text: "NEXT: 10 SECONDS INTO THE FUTURE"]**
