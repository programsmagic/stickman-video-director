But turning sensory inputs into numbers is only half the battle. How does an AI know that the visual image of a dog and the written word "D-O-G" represent the exact same concept? Enter CLIP: Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training. Imagine a massive high-dimensional sphere. CLIP takes hundreds of millions of image-and-caption pairs from the internet. When an image of a dog matches its caption, a mathematical gravity well pulls their vector points together on the sphere. When they don't match, an electrostatic force repels them apart. After billions of contrastive adjustments, CLIP creates a universal coordinate space. A photo of an apple, a line drawing of an apple, and the word "apple" in thirty different languages all share the exact same geometric address. Concepts became universal, independent of the senses.
