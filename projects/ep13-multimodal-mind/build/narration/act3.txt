Slicing images into words was a massive leap forward. But what about human speech? Sound isn't a static grid of pixels; it is a chaotic, continuous wave of vibrating air pressure. If you feed raw audio waveforms directly into an attention network, a single second of audio contains tens of thousands of pressure samples, choking the processor. The breakthrough came by turning sound into sight. Models like OpenAI's Whisper run a mathematical Fourier transform over the audio wave, decomposing the sound into eighty distinct frequency bands across time. This creates a Mel-spectrogram—a visual heatmap of sound where horizontal distance represents time, vertical height represents pitch, and brightness represents volume. To the machine, your spoken words become an image! The sensory walls were collapsing: hearing had become seeing, and seeing had become reading.
