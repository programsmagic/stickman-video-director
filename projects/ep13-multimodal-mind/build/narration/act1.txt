When you watch a thunderstorm roll across the horizon, your brain doesn't hesitate. You see the flash of violet lightning, you hear the low rumble of thunder, and you read the word "storm" on your weather app. To your mind, these are not three separate realities. They are a single, unified experience. But for seventy years, artificial intelligence was trapped in a Tower of Sensory Babel. Computer vision lived in its own isolated world of convolutional pixel filters. Natural language processing lived in a world of dictionary vocabulary tokens. And speech recognition lived in a world of acoustic waveform frequencies. If you wanted an AI to look at a photo and talk to you about it, you had to glue three separate Frankenstein models together in an awkward, laggy pipeline. How did we shatter the sensory walls? How did machines learn to see, hear, and speak through a single mind?
