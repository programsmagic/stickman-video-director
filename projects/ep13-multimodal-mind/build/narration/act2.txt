The revolution began with an audacious question: What if an image is secretly just a sentence in disguise? For decades, everyone believed images required convolutional neural networks—sliding tiny mathematical magnifying glasses over pixels to detect edges and curves. But in 2020, researchers introduced the Vision Transformer, or ViT. Instead of sliding filters, the model takes an image and brutally slices it into a grid of sixteen-by-sixteen pixel square patches. The top-left corner is patch number one. The eagle's eye is patch forty-two. The wingtip is patch one hundred. Each patch is flattened into a list of numbers and projected into an embedding vector. To the transformer's attention mechanism, these image patches look identical to words in a sentence! Suddenly, the exact same mathematical engine that reads Shakespeare could read an image.
