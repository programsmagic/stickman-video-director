On day four, you refuse to be passive. You decide you're going to break the rule. At the city library, a college student is studying alone at a wooden desk. Her timer: thirty-five minutes. You sit two tables away, watching the seconds melt. At ten minutes left, she packs her laptop into her canvas bag, stands up, and walks toward the exit. You follow her.

She steps onto the crosswalk. A city bus turns the corner fast, blinding rain on the windshield. You don't think. You sprint forward, grab her backpack, and yank her backward onto the sidewalk. The bus roars past, spraying puddle water across her jeans. She stumbles, gasping, furious and terrified: What is wrong with you?! Let go of me!

You look at her forehead. The timer didn't reset to eighty years. It didn't disappear. The digits simply shifted by sixteen minutes: Zero hours. Sixteen minutes. Twenty-eight seconds. Your stomach drops into ice water. You didn't cure mortality. You merely postponed the collision. Death wasn't a specific bus on a specific street. It was an inevitable biological and physical certainty woven into the fabric of time. By day five, after shadowing her and watching her collapse from an undiagnosed aneurysm in a pharmacy, you understand: You cannot cheat the ledger. You can only torture yourself trying to outrun it.
