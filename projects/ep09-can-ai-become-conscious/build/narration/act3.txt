This brings us to what neuroscientists call Qualia: the raw, subjective feeling of an experience. Consider the difference between detecting red light and experiencing red. A digital camera on Fifth Street can measure electromagnetic radiation at six hundred and fifty nanometers with microscopic accuracy. But the camera does not see beauty. It does not feel the warmth of a sunset. To illustrate this, philosophers invented the concept of a Philosophical Zombie. Imagine a creature that looks, talks, and behaves exactly like a human being. If you pinch its arm, it yelps in pain. If you tell it a joke, it laughs. If you ask it: "Are you conscious?", it replies: "Yes, I feel everything deeply." Yet, if you opened its skull, there is nobody home. There is no inner movie playing. No subjective sensation of joy, sorrow, or pain. The lights are on, but the house is completely empty. When an AI tells you it cares about your feelings, is there a genuine light burning inside, or are we projecting our own soul onto a mirror?
