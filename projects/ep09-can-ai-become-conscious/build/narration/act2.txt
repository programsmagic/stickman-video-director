In nineteen eighty, philosopher John Searle proposed one of the most famous thought experiments in history: The Chinese Room. Imagine you are locked inside a windowless room with only a tiny mail slot. You do not speak, read, or understand a single word of Chinese. Outside the room, a native Chinese speaker writes a question on a card, like "What is the meaning of friendship?", and slides it through the slot. Inside the room, you have an enormous English instruction manual. The book says: "If you see this squiggly symbol, go to page eight hundred, find this other squiggly symbol, write it on a piece of paper, and slide it back out." You follow the instructions perfectly. To the person standing outside, the answers coming through the slot are brilliant, witty, and fluent. They conclude: The mind inside this room is a genius! But did you understand Chinese? Of course not. You were just matching shapes. Searle argued that this is all a large language model is doing. Syntax is not semantics. Manipulating symbols is not understanding.
